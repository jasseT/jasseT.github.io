About early user testing

11.10.2024 J. Tamminen

I just recently had the great privilege to visit the wonderful people at Platform6, who were hosting a usability and early user testing workshop. I went there with a mindset of finding out what the sentiment of the startup community is towards usability and user testing. To my mind it’s a complete no-brainer. Of course you need to do user analysis, of course you need to talk to ypur customers, of course you need to test early, of course you need to iterate the design based on the feedback. And of course you have at least one person dedicated to doing that. Right? You do, don’t you? Why are you looking at me like I’m speaking in an alien language?

Look now. Technology is cheap. It’s relatively simple. Almost anyone can put together a web app pretty quickly, and if they can’t, they can ask AI. Same pretty much goes for design as well. So what’s the catch? What is the edge that makes your solution stand out?

The world is full of bad, really bad, usability. Yes, I’m looking at you, the developer who says: “They should just read the manual”. The world is full of apps which provide a very clever solution to a problem nobody has ever had. And what’s worse, the world is full of plugins for various platforms, which provide a very clever solution to a problem that shouldn’t exist. In other words, to a problem that was most likely created by poor design.

So why don’t we all start solving problems that are really there, instead of those we make up ourselves? Because we don’t know what those problems are. I certainly don’t. And chances are, if you are a developer, you don’t know either. Who does? A consultant at 3 000 € per day perhaps. Or not. You already know where I’m heading with this, because you’re clever. Yes, it’s the customer I’m talking about. They will know what problems they have, and you can find it out if you just go and ask. If you know how to ask the right questions, the customer might even provide you with a solution to that problem. After that it’s just a question of clever implementation.

And finally a disclaimer, I realise it might seem I’m downplaying the role of developers and development here, but I assure you I do not. As a general rule I don’t code, I don’t like it, I’m not very good at it, and I greatly respect the people who do it. But in my opinion these are things that coders should advocate as well. I think the job of a programmer would be a lot easier, if they were given a good, properly thought out and most importantly tested design document, on which they can base their work.
